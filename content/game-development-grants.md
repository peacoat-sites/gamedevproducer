Public money for games exists in more places than most developers realize, but it is scattered across national film institutes, provincial agencies, tax authorities and engine makers, each with its own rules. This directory pulls 22 of the most useful programs into one place, with what each one funds, how much, who can apply and when.

Every entry was checked against the official program page or the funder's own announcements in October 2026. Filter by region, type and stage, or search for your engine or province.

## Grants, tax credits and rebates: which is which

**Grants** are competitive. You apply, a panel or program officer assesses your project against the funder's goals, and successful applicants receive funding, often in stages tied to milestones. The UK Games Fund, Canada Media Fund, Creative Europe MEDIA, the CNC's FAJV, Germany's federal games funding and Epic MegaGrants all work this way.

**Tax credits and rebates** are rules-based. If your company and your spending qualify, you claim a percentage of what you spent, usually after the fact through your tax return or a rebate application. The UK's VGEC, Ireland's Section 481A credit, France's CIJV, the Canadian provincial credits, Australia's Digital Games Tax Offset and New Zealand's GDSR are in this group. They reward real spending in the country, so they do not help before you have money to spend, but they can be the largest source of public funding over a full production.

Many studios stack the two. Most programs cap the share of a project that public money can cover: Ontario Creates funds up to 50% of Ontario costs, Creative Europe up to 60% of eligible costs, and the CNC caps its fund at 50%. Every program asks you to disclose other public funding.

## How to read eligibility

Three questions decide most of it.

1. **Where is your company based, and who controls it?** Almost every public program is tied to a country, province or state, and some require local ownership and control as well as a local address.
2. **What stage are you at?** Prototype and development funds (such as the CMF Prototyping Program or Ontario's Concept Definition stream) need a different pitch from production funds, and tax credits only pay on money actually spent.
3. **Is there a cultural or content test?** The UK credit requires certification as British by the BFI, Ireland's requires a cultural certificate, and funds like Creative Europe look for cultural and cross-border value. Engine grants check what you are building with.
