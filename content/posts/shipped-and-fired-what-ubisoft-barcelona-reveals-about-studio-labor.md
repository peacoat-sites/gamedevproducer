Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced launched on July 9, 2026, and sold more than 2 million copies in its first 24 hours; within two weeks, Ubisoft said, it had exceeded its expectations for the whole year. Ubisoft Barcelona helped build it, working on main quests, the Gibara region, enemy and boss AI, and the underwater exploration. As the game broke records, the same studio was fighting 51 proposed layoffs, part of a restructuring Ubisoft had put forward in the weeks before launch.

If you have ever told yourself that things will stabilize once the game ships, this story is a direct challenge to that belief.

## What happened

- The 51 proposed layoffs at Barcelona were part of a wider Ubisoft cost plan affecting about 380 jobs, with the Winnipeg and Belgrade studios closing and the Barcelona office being restructured to focus on Rainbow Six, according to VGC's reporting.
- Workers, organized with the Coordinadora Sindical del Videojuego (CSVI) union, held a three-day strike in July. Their demands: cancel the layoffs, strengthen job security against future mass layoffs, restore the studio's previous policy of up to 60% remote work, and deliver promotions and raises that had already been promised.
- Ubisoft said the restructuring remained a proposal pending the outcome of the collective consultation process, and that it was committed to constructive dialogue.

It happened in a brutal month for the industry. On July 6, Xbox announced about 3,200 job cuts and spun off or sold four of its studios; we covered [what independence means for Double Fine and Compulsion](/posts/what-going-independent-actually-means-for-double-fine-and-compulsion-g/). And GDC's 2026 State of the Game Industry survey found that 28% of developers had been laid off in the previous two years, rising to a third in the United States.

## A hit is not a shield

A game selling 2 million copies on its first day does not protect the people who made it, and that is not as paradoxical as it sounds.

Restructurings at this scale are planned at the parent-company level, across many studios and sites, usually long before the announcement. The revenue from a hit flows into the publisher's results; what an individual studio costs is a separate line on a separate plan. A strong launch can make the optics of cuts worse without changing the plan behind them.

That matters for how you think about your own career. Working on a high-profile, commercially successful project feels like the safest place to be. In a restructuring, it is no safer than anywhere else in the company.

## What the strike shows about labor protections

Spanish law gave the Barcelona workers something most US developers do not have: collective layoffs at that scale require a formal consultation period with worker representatives, which gives a union time and a seat at the table. The CSVI used it.

In the United States, protections are thinner and depend on the employer, the state and whether a union contract exists. Unions have formed under the Communications Workers of America at studios including Raven Software, ZeniMax and several Blizzard teams, but most game workers are still not covered by a contract. Wherever you work, the law and any contract you are covered by decide what happens to you in a layoff far more than the success of your last game.

## What developers should know before a restructuring

You cannot control a parent company's cost plan. You can control how exposed you are when it lands:

- **Know your baseline.** Read your contract and your employer's policies for notice periods, severance, benefits continuation and what happens to unvested bonuses or equity. Knowing it in advance is information, not paranoia.
- **Know your local law.** Collective consultation in Spain and much of Europe, notice requirements for large layoffs in the US, and state rules all differ. Find out what applies to you.
- **Keep your portfolio current.** Document what you shipped and owned while it is fresh. For producers, that means milestones, scope decisions and outcomes; see [what to show hiring managers](/posts/portfolio-tips-for-aspiring-game-producers/).
- **Build a runway.** Even a few months of savings changes how you negotiate severance and how you choose your next job.

None of this fixes an industry where commercial success and job security have come apart. But the Barcelona workers named the problem clearly and used the protections they had, which is more than most.
