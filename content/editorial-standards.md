Game Dev Producer is written by [Stephen Brenish](/stephen-brenish/), a game producer who currently works at Epic Games. This page explains how the site is put together, so you can judge how much weight to give what you read here.

## Who writes the site

Stephen is the site's only author. He sets the topics, the point of view and the standards below. Views on this site are his own and don't represent Epic Games. Where a page discusses Unreal Engine or compares it with other engines, it says so, because Epic makes Unreal.

There is no wider editorial staff, and the site doesn't publish guest posts or invented contributor names.

## How AI is used

Articles are drafted with the help of AI tools, and every article says so at the end. That drafting works under fixed rules:

- **No invented experience.** Articles must not describe events, projects, conversations or results that didn't happen, and must not put first-person stories in Stephen's mouth that he didn't tell.
- **No invented numbers or sources.** Statistics, prices, dates and quotes need a named, checkable source. If a figure can't be sourced, it's left out.
- **An automated fact-check pass** runs on each new draft before it's published, looking for unsupported figures, citations and first-person claims.

Where Stephen has written something personally, it's attributed to him by name.

## What counts as a source

Facts come from primary or close-to-primary sources wherever possible: platform documentation (Steamworks, console certification guidelines, engine licensing terms), government and agency programs (for grants and tax credits), published postmortems and talks, and named industry research such as GameDiscoverCo. Trade press is used for news, with the outlet named. Each article lists its sources so you can check them.

When sources disagree, the article says so rather than picking whichever number sounds best.

## How the calculators and tools work

The site's [tools](/tools/), such as the Steam wishlist calculator, the budget calculator and the grants directory, are built on published data, and each tool names its sources and assumptions on the page. They produce estimates for planning, not guarantees. If a source changes, for example a platform fee or a grant deadline, the tool is updated.

## Keeping pages current

Articles show when they were last updated. Time-sensitive pages, such as engine pricing, Steam event dates and grant programs, are rechecked when the underlying rules change. Older articles that no longer hold up are updated, merged into a better page or removed.

## Money and independence

The site may earn money from Amazon affiliate links and display advertising. Those don't decide what's recommended. The site doesn't accept sponsored articles or paid placements.

## Corrections

If you find a factual error, use the [contact page](/contact/) and include the page address, the claim and your source. Confirmed errors are corrected, and substantive corrections are noted on the page.

## What the site doesn't do

- It doesn't give personal legal, tax or business advice. Contract and funding articles explain how things generally work; talk to a lawyer or accountant about your own deal.
- It doesn't review games for fun or quality. The focus is production and business.
- It doesn't report rumors about unannounced games or people.
